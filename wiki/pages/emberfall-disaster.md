**The Emberfall** was the disaster that began the calendar: years in [[Embervale]] are counted *After the Fall*, and the game is set in year 800. The game itself is named after it.

There are two versions of what happened. The **official story** is the one every child in Embervale is taught. The **true story** is what players slowly uncover. Writers: anything an NPC says out loud should match the official story, unless that NPC knows the secret.

## What everyone agrees on

These parts are true in both versions:

- Before the Fall, the land was [[Aldmera]], the kingdom of the [[Aldmere]], ruled from [[Whitestone]] by [[King Aldemar XVIII]].
- The king led [[The Ember Circle]], the kingdom's greatest mages. His two highest were [[Archmage Vale]] and [[Wick]].
- One night, deep under [[Heartvale]], a torrent of raw ember burst up out of the earth in a pillar of flame, and embers rained down across the land for about a year.
- King Aldemar went down to the fire and gave his life to hold it back. It was only a bandage: it held the torrent long enough for the Circle to build something lasting.
- The ground around the pillar fell in, leaving the round valley that is Heartvale today. Whitestone burned, and Aldmera was gone within the year.
- The surviving Circle came out of it changed: filled with power, and no longer ageing. They built [[The shrines]] to pin the torrent down, and the shrines began raising [[Emberborn]] about thirty years later.

## The official story

This is what [[The Emberguard]] teach, what the history books say, and what almost everyone in Embervale believes.

1. **Before:** Aldmera was a golden age, and the Circle its pride. Good King Aldemar led them, with Vale and Wick at his side.
2. **The Fall:** one night the land's fire woke under the valley. Nobody knows why. "The land's fire is wild, as all fire is."
3. **The king's sacrifice:** King Aldemar went down into the fire and poured his own ember into it to hold it back. It cost him his life, and it was only a bandage, but it held for about a year while the embers fell.
4. **The shrines:** that year gave Archmage Vale and the Circle time to build the lasting fix. Each raised a great shrine to pin the torrent down for good, and Vale raised the greatest, the Heart Shrine. About thirty years later the shrines began raising Emberborn, and the Circle spent two hundred years rebuilding the land.
5. **Wick's betrayal:** four hundred years later, Wick turned half the Circle against the rest and tried to tear the shrines down, which would have let the fire loose again. That was the [[Sundering War]]. Vale won it, and gave his life doing so.
6. **After:** Wick fled into the dark and was never seen again. The Circle's work passed to the [[Emberwardens]], who keep the great shrines and carry the old names as titles. In [[The Emberhall]], the king's statue stands by the Heart Shrine, and Wick's plinth stands empty, his name scratched off.

The lesson people take from it: the king died so the shrines could be built, the shrines are what stands between them and another Fall, the Emberguard are the land's protectors, and anyone who wants the shrines torn down is doing Wick's work.

## The true story

The fire didn't wake on its own. The Circle woke it, and Vale made it break loose.

1. **The working:** the Circle really did mean well. Aldemar's plan was to harness the Emberheart gently, so its warmth would reach every corner of Aldmera. The [[Ember Crown]] was the focus that let the king steer it, and the [[Ember Seed]] was what the working was meant to make.
2. **Vale's secret:** Vale alone suspected how much power the working could give one person, and he wanted it. As the work concluded, standing in the ring around the pit, he twisted the working so the power poured into the Circle instead of the land, the greatest share into himself.
3. **The burst:** the twisted working couldn't hold. The Emberheart's fire burst up through the shaft. The Circle stood at the source and it poured through them. Every member was filled with power and stopped ageing. Vale got the most.
4. **The king's sacrifice is real:** Aldemar saw what was coming and stepped into the shaft. He poured his own ember, and the steering power of the [[Ember Crown]], into the torrent to hold it back. It burned him away, but it held for a year: long enough for the Circle to raise the shrines. Vale took the crown from where the king fell.
5. **Only Vale knows:** the others had their eyes on the working, not on him. They believe it simply went terribly wrong. Wick believed it too, for four hundred years.
6. **The shrines:** the Circle built a great shrine in each region, one per member, to catch the wild ember and turn it into Emberborn: protectors to stabilize and guard the ruined land. They meant it. Over the centuries, the power they lived on corrupted them, and they began using the shrines and the Emberborn for their own ends (see [[History]]).
7. **The cover-up:** after the Sundering War, Vale rewrote history. He erased the working from every book, so nobody would ever ask whether the Circle caused the Fall, and the Emberfall became a disaster with no cause. He made Wick the traitor who tried to tear the shrines down. He faked his death, and the Undying took on new faces as the Emberwardens. The shaft is still there today, sealed under [[The Emberhall]].

### How the lie works

The official story isn't all made up. The king's sacrifice is true, and so are the shrines. What's missing is why the fire woke, and who Wick really fought.

| Question | Official story | Truth |
|---|---|---|
| What caused the Emberfall? | Nobody knows. The land's fire woke | The Circle's working to harness the Emberheart, twisted by Vale |
| How did the king die? | Holding the fire back, so the shrines could be built | The same. But he was cleaning up Vale's crime |
| Did the Circle know? | There was no working to know about | They know the working failed. Only Vale knows why |
| Who are the shrines for? | Everyone; they hold the torrent back | They were. Now they feed [[The Undying]] |
| What started the Sundering War? | Wick tried to tear the shrines down | Wick found out what the [[Radiance Ceremony]] really does |
| What happened to Vale? | Died winning the war | Faked his death. He is [[Enchanter Vale]], and he runs the Emberguard |
| Who are the Emberwardens? | Heirs who carry the old Circle names as titles | The same immortals, or impostors who took dead members' names |
| Is it over? | Yes, long ago | No. The land far from the shrines is still withering |

### Clues players can find

- The Circle's mark, a ring of fire, carved over the Emberhall's front door and on a ruined mage tower in [[The Ashen Barrens]]. It's the ring they stood in around the pit.
- The frozen shrine in [[Frostfell]], Wick's own shrine, with every member's name carved on it, Wick's included and unscratched.
- A page cut from the oldest book in the Emberhall library: an account of the working, written before the Sundering War. It's the only proof the Circle was working on the Emberheart at all.
- The *Old Shaft* Memory Echo: robed mages in a ring around a pit of fire, one of them twisting the flames toward himself, and the king stepping down into the pit.
- [[The Mages' Guild]]'s records about the Emberheart, which stop around the end of the Sundering War.
- Nobody knows where Vale is buried, and the Emberhall's Enchanter Vale is "very old, some say older than the Guild".

### When players learn it

Initiates only hear the official story. Players piece the truth together through quests, books and Memory Echoes. At the end of Act 3, after Rhys is drained, Wick tells them his side: the Circle lives, the shrines feed them, and Wick was framed. At their Joining, Vale confirms it himself. Even Wick has only guessed what Vale did; players prove it in the endgame (see [[Main story]]).
