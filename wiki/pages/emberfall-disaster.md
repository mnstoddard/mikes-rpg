**The Emberfall** was the disaster that began the calendar: years in [[Embervale]] are counted *After the Fall*, and the game is set in year 800. The game itself is named after it.

There are two versions of what happened. The **official story** is the one every child in Embervale is taught. The **true story** is what players slowly uncover. Writers: anything an NPC says out loud should match the official story, unless that NPC knows the secret.

## What everyone agrees on

These parts are true in both versions:

- Before the Fall, the land was [[Aldmera]], the kingdom of the [[Aldmere]], ruled from [[Whitestone]] by [[King Aldemar XVIII]].
- The king led [[The Ember Circle]], the kingdom's greatest mages. His two highest were [[Archmage Vale]] and [[Wick]].
- The Circle spent years on a great working: to harness [[The Emberheart]], the source of [[The ember]], so Aldmera would never know hunger, cold or sickness again.
- On the night they finished it, deep under [[Heartvale]], the king died and the working broke loose. A torrent of raw ember burst up out of the earth in a pillar of flame, and embers rained down across the land for about a year.
- The ground around the pillar fell in, leaving the round valley that is Heartvale today. Whitestone burned, and Aldmera was gone within the year.
- The surviving Circle came out of it changed: filled with power, and no longer ageing. They built [[The shrines]] to pin the torrent down, and the shrines began raising [[Emberborn]] about thirty years later.

## The official story

This is what [[The Emberguard]] teach, what the history books say, and what almost everyone in Embervale believes.

1. **Before:** Aldmera was a golden age, and the Circle its pride. Good King Aldemar led them, with Vale and Wick at his side.
2. **The betrayal:** Wick was jealous of the king and hungry for the power the working would bring. At the last moment he struck the king down and seized the working for himself. It broke loose, and that was the Emberfall. **Wick did this.**
3. **The rescue:** Archmage Vale and the loyal Circle held the torrent back with the shrines, raised the first Emberborn, and spent two hundred years rebuilding the land.
4. **Wick's second betrayal:** Wick hid his crime for four hundred years. When the Circle finally found him out, he turned half of them against the rest and tried to tear the shrines down. That was the [[Sundering War]]. Vale won it, and gave his life doing so.
5. **After:** Wick fled into the dark and was never seen again. The Circle's work passed to the [[Emberwardens]], who keep the great shrines and carry the old names as titles. Vale is remembered as the land's saviour. In [[The Emberhall]], Wick's plinth stands empty, his name scratched off.

The lesson people take from it: the shrines are what stands between them and another Fall, the Emberguard are the land's protectors, and anyone who wants the shrines torn down is doing Wick's work.

## The true story

Wick didn't kill the king. Vale did.

1. **The working:** the Circle really did mean well. Aldemar's plan was to harness the Emberheart gently, so its warmth would reach every corner of Aldmera. The [[Ember Crown]] was the focus that let the king steer it, and the [[Ember Seed]] was what the working was meant to make.
2. **Vale's secret:** Vale alone suspected how much power the working could give one person, and he wanted it. As the work concluded, standing in the ring around the pit, he killed Aldemar XVIII and twisted the working so the power poured into the Circle instead of the land, the greatest share into himself.
3. **The burst:** the twisted working couldn't hold. The Emberheart's fire burst up through the shaft and kept pouring out for a year. The Circle stood at the source and it poured through them. Every member was filled with power and stopped ageing. Vale got the most.
4. **Only Vale knows:** the others had their eyes on the working, not on him. They believe it simply went terribly wrong, and that the king died in the burst. Wick believed it too, for four hundred years.
5. **The shrines:** the Circle built a great shrine in each region, one per member, to catch the wild ember and turn it into Emberborn: protectors to stabilize and guard the ruined land. They meant it. Over the centuries, the power they lived on corrupted them, and they began using the shrines and the Emberborn for their own ends (see [[History]]).
6. **The cover-up:** after the Sundering War, Vale rewrote history. He took his own crime and gave it to Wick. He faked his death, and the Undying took on new faces as the Emberwardens. The shaft is still there today, sealed under [[The Emberhall]].

### How the lie works

The official story isn't all made up. It's the true story with Vale and Wick swapped.

| Question | Official story | Truth |
|---|---|---|
| Who killed King Aldemar? | Wick | Vale |
| What caused the Emberfall? | Wick seized the working | Vale twisted the working to take its power |
| Did the Circle know? | They found Wick out four hundred years later | No one but Vale has ever known |
| Who are the shrines for? | Everyone; they hold the torrent back | They were. Now they feed [[The Undying]] |
| What started the Sundering War? | Wick tried to tear the shrines down | Wick found out what the [[Radiance Ceremony]] really does |
| What happened to Vale? | Died winning the war | Faked his death. He is [[Enchanter Vale]], and he runs the Emberguard |
| Who are the Emberwardens? | Heirs who carry the old Circle names as titles | The same immortals, or impostors who took dead members' names |
| Is it over? | Yes, long ago | No. The land far from the shrines is still withering |

### Clues players can find

- The Circle's mark, a ring of fire, carved over the Emberhall's front door and on a ruined mage tower in [[The Ashen Barrens]]. It's the ring they stood in around the pit.
- The frozen shrine in [[Frostfell]], Wick's own shrine, with every member's name carved on it, Wick's included and unscratched.
- A page cut from the oldest book in the Emberhall library: an account of the working written before the Sundering War, that never mentions Wick doing anything wrong.
- The *Old Shaft* Memory Echo: robed mages in a ring around a pit of fire, and one of them stepping toward the king.
- [[The Mages' Guild]]'s records about the Emberheart, which stop around the end of the Sundering War.
- Nobody knows where Vale is buried, and the Emberhall's Enchanter Vale is "very old, some say older than the Guild".

### When players learn it

Hopefuls only hear the official story. Players piece the truth together through quests, books and Memory Echoes. At the end of Act 3, after Rhys is drained, Wick tells them his side: the Circle lives, the shrines feed them, and Wick was framed. At their Joining, Vale confirms it himself. Even Wick has only guessed who killed the king; players prove it in the endgame (see [[Main story]]).
