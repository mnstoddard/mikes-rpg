**The Emberfall** was the disaster that began the calendar: years in [[Embervale]] are counted *After the Fall*, and the game is set in year 500. The game itself is named after it.

There are two versions of what happened. The **official story** is the one every child in Embervale is taught. The **true story** is what players slowly uncover. Writers: anything an NPC says out loud should match the official story, unless that NPC knows the secret.

## What everyone agrees on

These parts are true in both versions:

- Fire burst up out of the earth under [[Heartvale]] in a great pillar of flame.
- Burning embers rained down across the [[Old Kingdom]] for about a year.
- The ground around the pillar fell in, leaving the round valley that is Heartvale today.
- Embers that landed in rock cooled into glowing shards worth more than gold. The kingdom's lords went to war over them: the [[Sundering War]].
- In year 40, [[Archmage Vale]] and his mages built [[The shrines]], the first [[Emberborn]] woke, and the war was won.

## The official story

This is what the Emberguard teach, what the history books say, and what almost everyone in Emberhold believes.

1. **Before:** the Old Kingdom was a golden age. The [[The Emberheart|land's fire]] burned quietly far below, and nobody thought about it.
2. **The Fall:** one day, with no warning, the land's fire *"burst in anger"*. Nobody knows why. Some say the old kingdom had grown proud, some say the land just does that. The point every teacher makes is the same: **no one was to blame.**
3. **The war:** greedy lords fought over the shards. *"No one now living remembers how the war began."* It ended with the king's crown lost, [[Brightcrown]] in ruins, and almost no one left.
4. **The rescue:** Archmage Vale, *"the greatest mage the land ever had"*, tapped the land's fire to build the shrines and gave the first heroes their bodies. The shrines tamed the land's anger and drove back [[The Blight]].
5. **After:** Vale died soon after the war. Nobody knows where he's buried. His loyal followers took up the work of keeping the shrines lit, and became [[The Emberguard]].

The lesson people take from it: the land's fire is dangerous, the shrines keep it calm, and the Emberguard are the land's protectors. Questioning the shrines is like asking for another Fall.

## The true story

The Emberfall wasn't natural, and it wasn't the land's anger. It was the first theft.

1. **Why:** [[The Ember Circle]] were the greatest mages of the Old Kingdom, led by [[Archmage Vale]]. They had power, but they were growing old. They wanted to live forever, and to be stronger than any king.
2. **The shaft:** in secret, they dug a great shaft down under Heartvale, all the way to [[The Emberheart]]. The shaft is still there today, sealed under [[The Emberhall]].
3. **The ritual:** standing in a ring around the pit, they cast a spell to drink from the Emberheart directly. This is the scene in the *Old Shaft* [[Memory Echoes|Memory Echo]]: robed mages chanting around a pit of fire.
4. **The burst:** the Emberheart can't be drunk from like a well. Its fire burst up out of their shaft and kept pouring out for a year. That's the pillar of flame and the rain of embers. The Circle nearly died, and the drink they did get was enough to keep them alive but not enough to make them immortal.
5. **The war:** the Circle let the lords fight over the shards and stayed quiet about who had started it all. They spent forty years working out a safer way to drink.
6. **The shrines (year 40):** their answer was the shrines: small, gentle taps all over the land that each draw a little ember and send it to them. To make it look like a gift, the shrines also shaped stolen ember into bodies, the first Emberborn. The Emberborn won the war, and the Circle became heroes.
7. **The cover-up (year 60 onwards):** Vale faked his death. The Circle renamed themselves the Emberguard, and their leaders became [[The Undying]]. They built the Emberhall over the shaft, wrote the history books, and around year 200 took [[The Mages' Guild]]'s records about the Emberheart. Five hundred years later, almost no one knows the Fall had a cause at all.

### How the lie works

The official story isn't all made up. It's the true story with the people taken out.

| Question | Official story | Truth |
|---|---|---|
| What caused it? | The land's fire burst in anger | The Ember Circle broke the earth open to drink the Emberheart |
| Who was to blame? | No one | Vale and the Circle |
| Why does nobody remember how the war began? | It was too long ago | The Circle made sure of it |
| Who are the shrines for? | Everyone; they calm the land's fire | The Undying; they drain the land's fire |
| What happened to Vale? | Died soon after the war, grave unknown | Faked his death in year 60. He is [[Enchanter Vale]], and he runs the Emberguard |
| Who are the Emberguard? | Vale's loyal followers, the land's protectors | The same Circle that caused the Fall, under a new name |
| Is it over? | Yes, 500 years ago | No. The shrines are a slow Emberfall. The [[The Blight|Blight]] is the land running dry |

### Clues players can find

- The Circle's mark, a ring of fire, carved over the Emberhall's front door and on a ruined mage tower in [[The Ashen Barrens]]. It's the same ring the mages stood in around the pit.
- The Circle's names, Vale's first, on the frozen shrine in [[Frostfell]], dated year 40.
- A page cut from the oldest book in the Emberhall library.
- The *Old Shaft* Memory Echo, and [[Old Maren]]'s memory of a young, smiling Vale in year 40.
- The Mages' Guild records about the Emberheart that stop around year 200.
- The carving on the Drowned Shrine off [[Saltmere Coast]]: *"The shrines shall close when the war is won."*
- Nobody knows where Vale is buried, and the Emberhall's Enchanter Vale is "very old, some say older than the Guild".

### When players learn it

Hopefuls only hear the official story. Players piece the truth together through quests, books and Memory Echoes, and get the whole of it on the day they're invited into the Emberguard. That's when they choose a side (see [[Main story]]).
